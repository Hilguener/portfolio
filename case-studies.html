Business Intelligence Work

These four dashboards were built for internal use. Since the underlying data belongs to the company, the write-ups below focus on the problem, the approach, and the outcome — not the live figures.

Tax Monitoring Dashboard

Power BISQLERP Integration

Status: Live

Problem

Tax obligations and compliance data lived inside the company's ERP system, but there was no consolidated way to monitor them. Checking tax status meant navigating the ERP directly or manually cross-referencing exports — slow, error-prone, and hard to keep current.

Approach

I connected directly to the ERP's underlying database and wrote SQL queries to extract and consolidate the relevant tax records. From there, I modeled the data in Power BI and built a dashboard focused on clarity: current obligations, due dates, and compliance status, refreshed from the source rather than manual exports.

Result

Tax obligations became visible in one place, sourced directly from the system of record instead of manual reconciliation — reducing the chance of something being missed or checked against stale data.

Operational Cash Flow BI

Power BISQLData Modeling

Status: Live

Problem

The company's chart of accounts hadn't been consistently maintained — transactions were classified inconsistently at the source, which meant any cash flow report built directly on top of it would be unreliable, regardless of how good the dashboard looked.

Approach

Instead of starting with the dashboard, I started with the data. I restructured the chart of accounts at the operational level and defined clear reclassification rules so transactions would be consolidated and categorized correctly at the source. Only after that foundation was solid did I build the Power BI dashboard on top of it, with measures for inflows, outflows, and period-based balances.

Result

The company now has a structurally reliable base for operational cash flow reporting — the kind of groundwork that matters more than the dashboard itself, since a clean visualization on top of messy data is still misleading.

Accounts Receivable & Payable Dashboard

Power BISQL

Status: Live

Problem

Receivables and payables data were scattered across different systems, with no consolidated view — making it hard to answer a simple question at a glance: what's coming in, what's due, and what the short-term cash position looks like.

Approach

I consolidated receivables and payables data from the source systems into a single Power BI dashboard, structuring it to show upcoming receipts and payments side by side, so the finance team could see short-term cash position at a glance instead of checking multiple sources.

Result

The finance team gained one consolidated view of receivables and payables, replacing scattered manual checks with a single reliable dashboard for day-to-day cash visibility.

Sales Commission & Goals Dashboard

Power BIDAXPostgreSQL

Status: Live

Problem

Sales commission was calculated manually in a spreadsheet — slow and error-prone. The underlying business logic was also non-trivial: goal attainment is measured against the month an invoice is issued, but commission is only paid once the payment is actually received, sometimes in installments spread across several months, always using the rate locked in at the original invoice month.

Approach

I built a star-schema semantic model directly on top of the ERP database, separating goals, invoiced revenue, and commission receipts into fact tables with their own grain and timing. I used DAX with time-shifted filter context to lock each installment's commission rate to its original invoice month, and documented the business rules and edge cases discovered while validating the numbers against the company's legacy reports.

Result

Replaced a manual, error-prone process with an automated, auditable dashboard — every commission value traceable line by line back to its source data.
